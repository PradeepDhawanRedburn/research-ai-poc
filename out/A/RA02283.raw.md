Evolution AB

Winter of Discontent

Thesis: A year ago, investors were expecting a 2H25-weighted inflection in growth as Evolution digested the twin growth headwinds of Asian cyberattacks and European ‘ringfencing’. But in our view, cyberattacks have now become a ‘forever war’, while our work suggests that further ringfencing headwinds are to come. We see downside risks to 2026E consensus expectations of a return to double-digit revenue growth.

**No ‘Sommar’ at all – ‘Winter of Discontent’ instead.** Our recent catch-up with Evolution suggested mixed trends (at best) during 3Q25. Early signs of stabilisation in Europe are potentially encouraging, but a recovery in Asian revenue growth remains distant. North American market growth remains tilted towards Evolution’s weak spot (random number generator (RNG) slots), while Latin America has promise, despite signs of near-term softness in Brazil.

**‘Tom & Jerry’ in Asia: Evolution’s own ‘forever war’.** Evolution characterised the impact of its cyberattack countermeasures as a bit of a ‘cat and mouse’ game – the bad actors hijacking the Evolution feed simply find ways to circumvent the new countermeasures implemented by the company. To us, the much hoped for revenue growth recovery in Asia remains as distant as ever. Meanwhile, gambling crackdowns in Asia continue at pace and add structural headwinds.

**European ringfencing checks indicate Evolution has fallen behind peers.** Our latest ringfencing checks in Europe have been expanded to examine the extent of Evolution’s ringfencing compared to other large industry peers such as **Hacksaw Gaming** (Not Rated) and **Pragmatic Play** (Not Listed). We see risks of Evolution ringfencing doubling from just seven countries to 14 in Europe – we do not believe this has been factored into consensus estimates.

**Reiterate Sell (price target: SEK610) – recent consensus cuts are not enough.** We argue that rebased consensus growth expectations still imply rebounds in group revenue growth to 9% and 10% in 2026E and 2027E respectively. But, for the reasons outlined above, we continue to see risks to consensus estimates. We trim our EBITDA estimates by 1%/3%/3% in 2025E/2026E/2027E, reflecting the more modest top-line trends. We reiterate our Sell rating and lower our price target to SEK610 (from SEK650).

Winter of our discontent

Earlier this month, we had a catch-up with the company discussing recent developments within the Evolution business. It is fair to say that trends remain mixed:

* **Asia** continues to be impacted by the cyberattacks/hijacking, where Evolution’s countermeasures are gradually circumvented. The company described its efforts here as a bit of a game of ‘cat and mouse’. To us, it appears that a reacceleration of revenue growth in Asia remains a far-off prospect.
* In **Europe**, the company expects some sequential improvement into 2H25 as the ringfencing takes hold. However, our ringfencing work in this report suggests that incremental headwinds continue to loom, with at least a further seven EU countries potentially subject to incremental ringfencing.
* In **Latin America**, Evolution remains at the mercy of the softer than expected market start in Brazil despite its longer-term promise. The company characterised the market as positive, despite being slow growing. This corresponds with recent commentary from Entain (Buy) management, which suggested that iGaming remains soft in Brazil, with recent growth being led largely by sports betting.
* In **North America**, the company has conceded that it remains a slots-driven market, and that the focus has turned to improving the RNG product in order to drive growth in the region.

Asia headwinds: no end in sight

To us, it appears that the revenue growth inflection in Asia is further away than ever – cyberattacks and gambling crackdowns are continuing to impact the business.

As a reminder, we remain sceptical about the impact that cyberattacks and hijacking are having on Evolution’s Asian business. In ‘[Regulatory Revolution](https://redburn.rothschildandco.com/pdf/63300?title=Regulatory+Revolution&pages=48)’ (17 January 2025), we argued that there was likely a confluence of both cyclical (cyberattacks) and structural (gambling crackdown) elements at play.

Nonetheless, the cyberattack narrative has now morphed from a cyclical/transitory one to a ‘forever war’, where revenue headwinds continue to impact the business as Evolution’s countermeasures are eventually circumvented by the content hijackers.

On the structural elements, as we discussed in ‘[Not Yet Sommar](https://redburn.rothschildandco.com/pdf/71126?title=Not+Yet+Sommar&pages=32)’ (24 July 2025), the regulatory crackdowns on online gambling continue across numerous jurisdictions, with the following developments over recent weeks:

* In **Japan**, on top of the previously reported arrests of end players, authorities have reportedly taken action against online casino advertising. There have been a number of developments:
  + Two individuals were [arrested](https://asianews.network/2-charged-with-abetting-us467-million-in-online-gambling-bets-in-japans-1st-crackdown-on-casino-ads-sites/) for promoting offshore gambling and receiving commissions from gambling sites, as authorities begin to target online affiliates in the country. This follows new laws that came into [effect](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2025/09/24/japan/crime-legal/gambling-law-takes-effect/) on 25 September 2025 that ban online casinos and prohibit social media platforms and websites from displaying gambling ads.
  + Moreover, authorities have even gone as far as [appealing](https://www.gamblingnews.com/news/japan-reaches-out-to-overseas-regulators-asking-for-assistance-in-curbing-illegal-gambling/) to regulators in Malta, Costa Rica, Georgia, Anjouan and Curaçao, among others, to seek assistance in curbing illegal online gambling offered by overseas operators in Japan. This mirrors efforts from regulators in Australia, which has sought similar assistance from overseas jurisdictions.
* **South Korea** has shut down an illegal online gambling operation that was based in Cambodia, which [reportedly](https://www.chosun.com/english/national-en/2025/09/10/2MWKPWJT6VHPBBSFVCWOM72VTM/) handled as much as KRW44bn in gambling flows. Ten individuals were arrested and a further 174 who played the games were charged. Games allegedly played included Baccarat*.*
* In **India**, which recently passed a new law banning all forms of real money gaming, the Enforcement Directorate of India has taken steps to seize funds linked to illegal online gambling in the country – targeting various sports stars and well-known actors who were [reportedly](https://www.newindianexpress.com/nation/2025/Sep/28/ed-to-seize-assets-of-celebrities-sportspersons-in-1xbet-betting-app-case-under-pmla) gambling on 1xBet. Evolution content is indeed available on 1xBet, while our industry contacts noted that the content was being distributed into the country via aggregators such as Diamond Exchange and BollyTech.
* **Vietnamese** authorities have reportedly [jailed](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/news/crime/vietnam-jails-43-in-3-8b-online-gambling-crypto-case-4943746.html) as many as 43 people in a $3.8bn illegal gambling case linked to crypto and money laundering, with offences dating back as far as 2020-21.
* In **Malaysia**, the gambling crackdowns [continue](https://tribuna.com/en/casino/news/2025-09-03-malaysia-authorities-raid-hundreds-of-illegal-gambling-premises-arrest-328-in-coordinated/), with 328 arrested in a large-scale operation as recently as August 2025.
* **Philippines** banned the Philippine Offshore Gaming Operators (POGOs) more than a year ago and continues its crackdown on operators that have failed to heed the new laws. 91 illegal Chinese workers were [deported](https://manilastandard.net/news/crime-courts/314645015/bi-deports-91-illegal-pogos-chinese-workers.html) with China’s cooperation.
* **Indonesia** has reportedly [taken down](https://en.antaranews.com/news/380477/govt-removes-28m-digital-posts-most-tied-to-online-gambling) up to 2.8 million posts on social media and online forums linked to illegal online gambling in the country since October 2024.

Regulatory ringfencing update

Our ringfencing updates show no material changes on the surface. The seven EU countries where Evolution appears to ringfence its products are: **Czech** **Republic**, **Denmark**, **Greece**, **Latvia**, **Netherlands**, **Romania** and **Sweden**. The well-documented UK Gambling Commission (UKGC) licence review also sees the **UK** as an eighth European market where Evolution ringfences.

However, we have expanded upon our checks to include peer B2B suppliers Hacksaw Gaming (Not Rated) and Pragmatic Play (Not Listed) in a comparison versus Evolution. This draws out some interesting observations with respect to potential incremental ringfencing risks for Evolution (Fig 3).

Can Evolution placate shareholders?

We believe Evolution will seek to placate shareholders at 3Q25 results via the announcement of a new c€500m share buyback over and above the existing programme. The existing buyback programme is on track for completion by November 2025 (Fig 14). However, we have not included this in our refreshed estimates.

Furthermore, with the existing Galaxy Gaming (GLXZ) acquisition still subject to a multitude of regulatory approvals (including Nevada), we suspect that Evolution may seek to withdraw from the deal and pivot towards returning the capital to shareholders instead. Galaxy’s share price continues to trade at a material discount to the $3.20/share bid price, implying significant market scepticism that the deal will indeed close during 2H25 (Fig 15).

Withdrawing from the GLXZ deal would unlock a further c€100m (c$120m enterprise value) of capital that could be put towards a new share buyback programme in 2026.

However, this would raise a question around the regulatory issues Evolution faced that stalled the deal completion in the first place. If state regulators found reason to stall or delay regulatory approvals for GLXZ, what could this mean for Evolution’s prospects of securing incremental iGaming supplier licences should these same states eventually elect to legalise iGaming?

Consequently, we would regard Evolution’s failure to complete the GLXZ acquisition negatively given the longer-term implications for Evolution’s North American growth prospects.

Reiterate Sell rating

Given our below-consensus earnings estimates, we continue to see downside risk to Evolution’s share price. We cut our target price to SEK610 (from SEK650) and reiterate our Sell rating.

Upside risks to our valuation include a faster than anticipated recovery in growth, led by Asia and Europe. However, our ringfencing checks suggest that this much hoped for growth inflection appears ever distant, and we believe it will begin to test investor patience. We do not believe that a renewed share buyback will be enough to placate shareholders with respect to the possible negative implications for Evolution’s North American growth prospects.
